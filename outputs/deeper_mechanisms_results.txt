MECHANISM 1: RESTAURANT DENSITY (does it explain the borough gap?)
========================================================================

Community boards with density data: 69
Restaurants per board: min=1, median=309, max=3023

Paired re-inspections with a known community board: 14244
Raw correlation, log(board density) vs B/C outcome: r=-0.030

Baseline model (no density term), same covariates as analyze_reinspection_model.py:
      borough  odds_ratio  ci_low  ci_high  p_value
        Bronx       1.119   0.968    1.294    0.128
     Brooklyn       0.985   0.887    1.095    0.784
       Queens       1.116   1.005    1.239    0.041
Staten Island       0.879   0.714    1.081    0.220

With log(restaurant density) added as a covariate:
      borough  odds_ratio  ci_low  ci_high  p_value
        Bronx       1.098   0.911    1.323    0.328
     Brooklyn       0.975   0.863    1.102    0.688
       Queens       1.105   0.981    1.245    0.099
Staten Island       0.866   0.693    1.083    0.208

Density effect itself:
 odds_ratio  ci_low  ci_high  p_value
      0.988   0.918    1.063    0.744

Bronx OR: 1.119 -> 1.098 after adding density (p 0.128 -> 0.328)
Queens OR: 1.116 -> 1.105 after adding density (p 0.041 -> 0.099)

Caveat: median board density is 1323 restaurants in Manhattan vs 439 elsewhere -- density is itself correlated with borough, so this attenuation is ambiguous. It could mean density is a real confound, or it could just be collinearity between density and borough inflating the standard errors without density doing real explanatory work. Both borough coefficients also widen and lose significance, consistent with either story. Verdict: suggestive, not conclusive -- density is the first candidate that visibly moves the estimate, but it can't be called a confirmed explanation.
